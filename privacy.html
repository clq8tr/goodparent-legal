Privacy Policy

The Good Company · Good Parent · Version 6, effective 2026-09-30

Your records live on this phone. Nothing is uploaded unless you switch something on and say yes.

This is a record-keeping tool. It is not legal advice, not medical advice, and not a lawyer.

You own everything you write. You can export all of it, any time, for free.

No ads, no analytics, no trackers. Nothing about you or your children is ever sold.

The short version

Everything you enter is stored on your device, in a database only this app can read.

Four features can send something off the device. Every one of them is off until you turn it on, every one names what it sends before you agree, and every one can be turned off again in a tap. They are listed in full below.

There are no ads, no analytics, no trackers, no advertising identifiers, no crash reporting, and no third-party marketing tools anywhere in this app.

We do not sell, rent, or share your personal information, and we never will. We do not "share" it for cross-context behavioural advertising as US state privacy laws use that word.

What is stored on your device

Your entries: custody events, journal notes, expenses and receipts, calendar events, documents you add, contacts, health entries such as allergies and medications, and growth measurements.

Information about your children that you choose to enter: names, nicknames, dates of birth, photos, school details, sizes, and anything you type into a note.

A local identifier generated on first launch, used to tie your records together on the device. It is not linked to your name, email or phone number, and it is not shared with anyone.

Your settings, your accepted-terms record, and — if you turned them on — a cached weather reading and a cached list of nearby places.

Stored documents are held behind your device lock (Face ID, Touch ID, fingerprint, face unlock or passcode) and are marked never to be uploaded, including in backups.

Everything that can leave your device, in full

ONE — An account and cloud sync. Off by default. Only if you sign in. Sends: your records, so they can be mirrored and restored to another device; and your email address, unless you use Apple’s Hide My Email, in which case we never receive one. Processed by Supabase on our behalf. Delete the account in the app and the server copy goes with it.

TWO — AI-assisted receipt reading. Off by default. Only when you tap it, and only after a consent screen naming the provider. Sends: the plain text your phone already recognised from a receipt. Never the photograph. Received by Anthropic, via our server, to answer that one request; it is not used to train a model.

THREE — Weather and nearby places. Off by default. Sends: your position rounded to roughly one kilometre. The app never asks your phone for a precise location at all. It sends nothing about your children — not a name, not an age, not a date of birth. Received directly by Open-Meteo (weather) and, for places, by Apple Maps on iPhone (searched through your phone’s own maps service) or OpenStreetMap’s Overpass service on Android and as a fallback; we have no server in between and never see where you are.

FOUR — Independent timestamping. Off by default, and a paid feature. Sends: a cryptographic hash of your record’s current state — a fixed-length fingerprint from which the underlying data cannot be recovered. Received by a public timestamp authority. No content is sent.

That is the complete list. With all four off, this app makes no network requests at all.

What we never collect

We do not collect analytics or usage statistics. We do not know how often you open the app, what you tap, or how many entries you have.

We do not collect crash reports. A stack trace from an app like this one can carry a child’s name, so crashes are logged on your device only, for you to read and share deliberately if you choose.

There is no advertising identifier, no tracking pixel, no fingerprinting, and no SDK from an advertising or analytics company anywhere in the build.

We do not track you across other apps or websites, and we do not answer "yes" to Apple’s tracking question because there is nothing to say yes about.

Information about children

Good Parent is a general-audience app for adults. Children do not use it, do not have accounts, and are never asked for anything.

You, the adult, may choose to record information about a child — a name, a date of birth, a photo, an allergy. That information is stored on your device under your control, and it is only ever transmitted if you have turned on cloud sync, in which case it is mirrored to your account and to nobody else.

We do not knowingly collect personal information directly from a child under 13. If we learn that a child has provided information to us directly, we will delete it promptly.

As the parent or guardian you can review, change, export or delete everything recorded about your child at any time, from inside the app, without asking us. Deleting your account deletes the server copy; resetting local data deletes the device copy.

We do not use children’s information for advertising, profiling, or any purpose other than making the features you asked for work.

Keeping it, and getting rid of it

On your device, your records are kept until you delete them. Records are append-only by design — editing an entry writes a new version and keeps the old one — so that a record cannot be quietly rewritten later. "Reset local data" removes all of it.

On our servers, a synced copy is kept while your account exists and is deleted when you delete your account. Deletion is prompt — a matter of days, not weeks — including from routine backups as they rotate.

Cached weather and place results are kept on your device for hours and days respectively, and are deleted immediately when you turn that feature off.

Your rights

Wherever you live, you can export everything in the app, in an open format, at any time, for free. That is portability, and it does not require asking us.

You can correct anything by editing it, and delete anything by deleting it. Both are in the app.

Depending on where you live — including under the GDPR and UK GDPR, the California Consumer Privacy Act, and similar US state laws — you may also have rights to access, correct, delete, restrict or object to processing, and to complain to a regulator. Because we hold nothing unless you signed in, the fastest route for most of these is the app itself; for anything else, contact us.

We will never discriminate against you for exercising a privacy right. There is no worse version of the app for people who ask.

Security

Records are stored in the app’s private storage on your device, protected by the operating system’s app sandbox and by your device lock.

Anything that does travel travels over encrypted connections (TLS). Where a copy is stored on our infrastructure, it is protected by row-level security so that an account can only ever reach its own rows.

Sign-in is passwordless — Apple, Google, or a single-use link sent to your email. There is no password for this app to store, to leak, or for anyone to reuse from another site that was breached.

No system is perfectly secure. The strongest protection here is structural rather than promised: for a user who never signs in, we hold nothing at all.

Where information is processed

If you use an account, your synced records are processed on infrastructure that may be located in the United States or elsewhere. By using that optional feature you understand that information may be transferred to and processed in a country other than your own.

If you do not use an account, nothing is transferred anywhere.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes materially, the app will ask you to read and accept the new version before you continue.
